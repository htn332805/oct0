## Source Material
The seventeenth story is "Chiếc Ghế Không Tựa Lưng" (The Chair Without a Backrest).

**Summary:**
The main character, Trầm Lão, is a skilled carpenter who makes many sturdy chairs but refuses to add backrests to them.
Buyers come back after a few days, complaining that sitting without a backrest is uncomfortable, so they ask him to add one.
Trầm Lão explains that chairs without backrests force people to sit upright and support themselves instead of leaning.
He believes that resting on a backrest can make people lazy and weak in their body and spirit.
One day, Quỷ Cốc Tử visits and sits on one of these chairs, observing and asking why Trầm Lão insists on making chairs this way.
Trầm Lão responds that he wants people to learn to support themselves, to take responsibility and build inner strength.
Quỷ Cốc Tử agrees that true strength comes not from relying on support but from standing up on one's own.
Over time, villagers get used to sitting without backrests, gaining better posture and discipline.
Trầm Lão dies, and the villagers honor him by placing a chair without a backrest at his grave.

**Lesson:**
The core teaching is that strength, responsibility, and resilience come from supporting oneself rather than relying too much on external supports.
Depending excessively on others or comforts can weaken one's inner strength and autonomy.
Learning to be self-reliant builds courage and personal growth.
The chair without backrest symbolizes disciplined independence—not punishment, but empowerment.
The story encourages embracing challenges that force growth rather than seeking ease that breeds weakness.

**Deeper Themes: "Chiếc Ghế Không Tựa Lưng"**

**Symbolism:**
- Chair Without a Backrest: Represents self-reliance, personal responsibility, and inner strength.
- Backrest: Symbolizes external support, comfort, and potential dependency.
- Sitting Upright: Reflects discipline, awareness, and active engagement with oneself.

**Themes**
1. Self-Reliance and Personal Responsibility
   - Sitting without a backrest requires one to support their own body and posture.
   - This physical metaphor extends to life's challenges—strong individuals support themselves without overdependence.

2. Risks of Over-Dependence
   - Relying heavily on external comforts or supports can weaken resilience and initiative.
   - Comforts can lead to complacency and diminish one's ability to face difficulties independently.

3. Discipline as Empowerment
   - The discomfort of sitting without support encourages awareness and discipline.
   - Discipline is framed not as punishment but as a means to cultivate deeper strength.

4. Growth Through Challenge
   - Challenges, even small ones like an uncomfortable chair, stimulate growth and adaptation.
   - Avoiding discomfort may stunt development.

5. Balance Between Support and Autonomy
   - While support is valuable, balance ensures it doesn't become crutching or erosion of self-capacity.
   - The story encourages cultivating abilities to stand firm before seeking aid.

**Practical Insights**
- Practice Self-Support: Engage in activities that build your ability to manage stress, emotions, and tasks independently.
- Limit Comfort-Seeking: Recognize when you are over-relying on comforts or supports that may hinder growth.
- Embrace Healthy Discomfort: See challenges and discomfort as opportunities for development.
- Build Discipline: Cultivate routines and habits that strengthen awareness, focus, and resilience.
- Evaluate Your Support System: Use support intentionally and avoid dependence.
- Reflect on Autonomy: Journal or meditate on areas in life where you can grow independence.

This story reminds us that true strength and growth arise from disciplined self-reliance, not from always leaning on others or external comforts.

## Phase 6: Elaborative Interrogation — Ask "Why?" Relentlessly
*(Deep questioning: 20 minutes)*

**Copilot Prompt Used:** *"For each of the following facts from my notes, generate a 'Why is this true?' and 'How does this work?' question."*

**Sample Questions and Answers:**
1. **Fact:** Sitting without a backrest requires supporting one's own body.
   - **Why is this true?** Because without external support, muscles must engage to maintain posture.
   - **How does this work?** The body activates core muscles and spine alignment naturally when no backrest is available.

2. **Fact:** Over-dependence on comforts weakens resilience.
   - **Why is this true?** Reliance on external aids reduces the need for personal effort and adaptation.
   - **How does this work?** Comfort creates complacency, diminishing the brain's problem-solving and stress-response mechanisms.
