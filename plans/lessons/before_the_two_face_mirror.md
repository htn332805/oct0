## Source Material
The third story is "Trước Gương Hai Mặt" (Before the Two-Faced Mirror).

**Summary:**
The main character is Tô Minh, a wealthy and eloquent merchant known as "Tô Minh Lưỡi Vàng" (Golden Tongue), who skillfully convinces and persuades others.
One day, an old beggar sells him a two-faced mirror: one face shows what he wants others to see, and the other reveals hidden, unpleasant truths he fears to face.
Tô Minh initially avoids looking at the second face, but over time, the hidden reflections haunt him: his hypocrisy, greed, betrayal, and loneliness.
Eventually, after years of wealth and fame, Tô Minh accepts the mirror's truth, chooses to merge the two faces into one, symbolizing living an authentic, honest life.
He closes his business, opens a small tea shop, and embraces a simple, sincere life, no longer wearing masks.

**Lesson:**
The core message is the harm of living a double life or wearing masks—pretending, hiding true self, or leading a divided existence.
Two-faced living creates inner conflict, loneliness, and loss of authenticity.
True peace and integrity come from embracing all parts of oneself, accepting flaws, and living honestly.
The merged mirror symbolizes unity of self—no more pretense, just genuine living.
The story calls for courage to face one's shadow side and choose wholeness over appearance.

**Deeper Themes: "Trước Gương Hai Mặt"**

**Symbolism:**
- Two-Faced Mirror: Represents the duality many people live with—the polished image shown to the world versus the hidden inner self.
- First Face: The public persona, socially acceptable, successful, and admired.
- Second Face: The concealed truths—flaws, fears, guilt, insecurities, and actions one may reject or deny.
- Merged Single Face: Symbolizes authenticity, acceptance, and integration of the whole self.

**Themes**
1. Duality and Self-Deception
   - Many hide behind carefully crafted images to gain approval, success, or love.
   - This dual life often involves self-deception and disconnection from true self.
   - The outer face is an act; the inner face contains what's genuinely felt or feared.

2. The Psychological Cost of Masks
   - Wearing masks can lead to loneliness, because deep bonds need authenticity.
   - The internal conflict between the two faces causes emotional suffering and alienation.
   - Ignoring or suppressing the "shadow" side intensifies fear and self-rejection.

3. Courage to Face One's Shadow
   - It takes bravery to look candidly at one's darker or hidden aspects.
   - This confrontation is necessary for growth and healing.
   - The mirror's two faces challenge Tô Minh to embrace the parts of himself he avoided.

4. Integration and Wholeness
   - True freedom comes from accepting and uniting all aspects of self.
   - The merged mirror is no longer fragmented; it reflects the whole person.
   - Authentic living means no longer having to perform or hide; it means being at peace with oneself.

5. Choosing Simplicity and Truth Over Appearance
   - After embracing his whole self, Tô Minh rejects material success for a simple, sincere life.
   - This reflects the deeper fulfillment that arises from honesty and authenticity.

**Practical Advice**
- Self-Reflection: Take time to honestly look at your own "two faces"—what parts do you show, and what do you hide?
- Embrace Imperfections: Accept that flaws and weaknesses are part of being human, and hiding them only breeds inner conflict.
- Practice Vulnerability: Share your authentic self with trusted people to build genuine relationships.
- Reduce Pretenses: Notice situations where you feel compelled to "perform" and try gradually to relax into being yourself.
- Seek Wholeness: Work on uniting conflicting parts within yourself, possibly through journaling, therapy, or honest conversations.
- Value Authenticity Over Approval: Prioritize living truthfully over gaining others' praise or admiration.
- Simplify Life: Reflect on areas where pursuing external success or image causes stress, and explore simplifying in favor of meaning.

## Phase 6: Elaborative Interrogation — Ask "Why?" Relentlessly
*(20 minutes: Generate explanations for why facts are true)*

**Selected Facts for Interrogation:**

1. **Fact:** Living a double life creates inner conflict.
   **Why is this true?** Because the brain has to constantly maintain two different versions of reality - what you show others vs. what you know inside. This creates cognitive dissonance, where conflicting beliefs cause stress and anxiety.
   **How does this work?** The prefrontal cortex works harder to suppress authentic thoughts while presenting the false persona, leading to mental fatigue and emotional exhaustion.

2. **Fact:** Authenticity leads to genuine relationships.
   **Why is this true?** People can sense when someone is being fake, even subconsciously. Authentic people attract others who value honesty, creating deeper emotional connections based on trust rather than performance.
   **How does this differ from superficial relationships?** Superficial bonds are built on mutual pretense and can dissolve when masks slip, while authentic relationships withstand challenges because they're based on real understanding.

3. **Fact:** Facing one's shadow side requires courage.
   **Why is this true?** Confronting hidden flaws, fears, and guilt triggers the brain's threat response, making it feel dangerous even though it's actually safe. This evolutionary response makes vulnerability feel risky.
   **How does this lead to growth?** By repeatedly facing fears in small doses, the brain learns they're not actually threatening, reducing anxiety and building resilience.
